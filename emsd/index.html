Automatic Bookshelf Managing System (ABMS)

An automated gantry–gripper–computer-vision system that can sort, store, and retrieve books on demand, designed to reduce manual work in high-volume libraries and collections.

Final ABMS prototype with 3-axis gantry and book-handling gripper mounted in front of a bookshelf.

📚 Project Overview

Traditional book management in libraries and large personal collections is labor-intensive and physically demanding: librarians must sort returned books, walk shelves to re-shelve items, and patrons still need to search manually for specific titles. The Automatic Bookshelf Managing System (ABMS) addresses this by automating sorting, placement, and retrieval of books using a Cartesian gantry, adaptive gripper, and computer-vision-based identification.

The system is designed around a 3-axis gantry carrying a rack-and-pinion gripper and a camera for QR-code-based book identification. It targets environments such as public/university libraries, bookstores, archives, and serious home libraries, where high circulation and large collections make manual handling costly and error-prone.

🧩 System Architecture

Subsystems

  • Gantry: 3-axis Cartesian robot built from 20×20 mm aluminum extrusions, driven by NEMA 17 stepper motors via lead screws (Z) and timing belts (X, Y).
  • Gripper: 2-plate rack-and-pinion design with servo actuation, rubberized contact surfaces, and a force-sensitive resistor (FSR) to cap gripping force and protect books.
  • Computer vision: Camera + OpenCV pipeline for QR code detection; each book is tagged and mapped to shelf coordinates.
  • Control & database: Python control stack communicating with an Arduino over serial, managing motions and a CSV-based book location map.

Store / Retrieve Flow

  • Store: Scan book QR → find free shelf gap → move gantry → grip → place → log book ID, shelf layer, X-coordinate, and thickness in CSV.
  • Retrieve: Look up book ID in CSV → move gantry to location → grip → bring to pickup point → update database.

🤖 Gripper & Mechanical Design

The gripper was identified as a critical subsystem: it must securely handle books of varying width and mass without damage, while staying compact enough to fit between shelves. Multiple concepts were generated (stag-beetle clamp, linkage-based parallel plates, rack-and-pinion variants) and evaluated using a decision matrix on stability, load capacity, cost, robustness, and ease of manufacturing.

A rack-and-pinion parallel-plate design was ultimately selected:

  • Driving force is aligned with plate motion, minimizing unwanted side loads and friction.
  • The mechanism delivers consistent gripping force across the motion range.
  • Structure is easy to prototype in PLA and later upgrade to machined aluminum.
  • Integrated square FSR pad (1–49 N) limits gripping force to below ~10 N for safety.

FEA results on critical parts (side walls, gear shaft, combined assembly) confirmed stresses and deflections are well below material limits with a safety factor of ~2 for the maximum 2.5 kg book load.

Rack-and-pinion gripper with symmetric plates, FSR integration, and servo actuation via gear train.

⚡ Electrical & Software Stack

The electrical system is built around a 110 V AC to 24 V DC supply feeding four TB6600 stepper drivers and NEMA 17 motors through 2 A fuses, with emphasis on modularity, protection, and thermal management. Limit switches on each axis ensure safe homing and prevent collisions.

  • Power: 110 V → 24 V supply, four 2 A fuses, parallel wiring to TB6600 drivers (800 steps/rev, 2 A max).
  • Motors: NEMA 17 stepper motors for gantry axes; servo motor for gripper plates to allow torque control and closed-loop positioning.
  • Sensing: Limit switches on linear actuators; FSR in gripper for contact force measurement.
  • Software: Python + pySerial for high-level commands, CSV-based storage database, Arduino firmware for low-level axis and gripper control.

🎥 Demonstration & Testing

The final prototype demonstrates a full store and retrieve cycle: homing sequence, book pickup from a designated drop-off corner, placement at a computed free slot, database update, followed by retrieval back to the pickup zone. Testing verified:

  • Accurate gantry motion to commanded shelf coordinates.
  • Stable gripping behavior with FSR-controlled force and rubberized contact surfaces.
  • Correct CSV updates for book insertion/removal, avoiding double-storage and tracking free gaps.

Limitations observed include sensitivity to very heavy / oversized books (due to PLA flexibility in the prototype) and testing in relatively ideal scenarios with few books. Future iterations would upgrade to aluminum structures, expand the test set, and integrate with full library inventory software.
