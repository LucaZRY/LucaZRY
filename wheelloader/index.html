Objective & Metrics

This project investigates whether reinforcement learning can produce smooth, reliable, and efficient scooping motions for a wheel loader in simulation. The emphasis is on repeatability under granular contact, while respecting actuator limits and discouraging unstable force/torque spikes.

Introduction

Wheel loaders execute repetitive scooping and loading cycles in construction and mining. These tasks are physically demanding and sensitive to operator skill, pile geometry, and contact dynamics. Autonomous scooping is challenging because it involves underactuated vehicle motion, articulated linkage dynamics, and contact-rich interaction with granular material.

To study the problem in a controlled setting, I built a high-fidelity simulation in Isaac Sim / IsaacLab with an articulated arm + bucket, realistic joint limits, and rock pile interaction. The core question is whether a learned policy can outperform a hand-designed motion baseline in bucket fill and stability while generalizing across pile variations.

System Pipeline

  • Simulation environment: articulated loader model + granular pile interaction + task reset/randomization.
  • Baseline motion: Bézier/scripted scooping trajectory for initialization and debugging.
  • RL training: PPO-style continuous control using reward shaping for fill, smoothness, and constraint compliance.
  • Evaluation: fixed settings + randomized pile tests; analyze fill proxy, stability, and failure modes.

Methods

Simulation & Environment Design

  • Built a custom Isaac Sim / IsaacLab environment for scooping with realistic joint/actuator limits and PD-based control.
  • Modeled rock piles as granular rigid bodies / particle-like sets with tuned friction/contact parameters.
  • Added scene assets (e.g., boundaries/walls) to control pile containment and encourage repeatable interactions.

Observations & Actions

  • Observations: joint positions/velocities, bucket pose, and task-relevant signals (e.g., contact cues / pile features as available).
  • Actions: continuous torque commands or joint velocity targets for arm and bucket joints (depending on controller mode).

Reward Shaping

  • Encouraged scooping success via a bucket-fill proxy (payload lifted / retained).
  • Penalized energy usage and high-frequency oscillation to promote smooth actuator profiles.
  • Added safety terms for joint limit violations, unstable impacts, and undesirable contacts.

Results

In simulation, the learned policy produced smoother and more consistent scooping behaviors than the scripted baseline. Qualitatively, the policy improved bucket penetration and reduced abrupt reversals that typically cause torque spikes.

  • Bucket fill: improved fill consistency across randomized piles (within training range).
  • Stability: reduced torque spikes and fewer oscillatory corrections during penetration/lift phases.
  • Robustness: better tolerance to moderate pile-shape variation compared to a fixed scripted motion.
Tip: one “fill vs time” plot + one torque-profile comparison image is usually very convincing.

Discussion

The results support the premise that reinforcement learning can improve scooping quality in a physics-based simulator, especially when the reward explicitly encodes stability and constraint compliance. The largest practical challenges were enforcing consistent control interfaces (PD vs torque modes), stabilizing contact dynamics for granular media, and designing reward terms that avoid pathological “hacks” while still providing learnable signal.

Future work includes stronger domain randomization for sim-to-real transfer, perception-driven conditioning (pile geometry from sensors), and integration with higher-level task planning (approach, align, scoop, lift, dump).
